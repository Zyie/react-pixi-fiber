# Differences from react-three-fiber

react-pixi-fiber runs react-three-fiber v10's core unchanged, so the React side of r3f works as r3f documents it. The differences are in what that core drives: pixi-3d objects in place of three.js ones. This page lists them, item by item, from the findings of the port.

## What is the same

Everything r3f does in React: `<Canvas>`, `createRoot`, `useFrame` and its phases, `useThree`, `useLoader` and Suspense, `invalidate` and `advance`, `frameloop`, `extend`, `createPortal`, the event system with bubbling, capture and `onPointerMissed`, and how props, `args` and `attach` reach objects. All of it is r3f's own code.

## What differs

The objects behind the elements are pixi-3d's. Declarative props work as in r3f, since the elements keep three's names and map them. What reaches past the props needs care.

### Scene graph

- **Imperative math and colours.** `position={[1, 2, 3]}`, `scale={2}` and `rotation={[x, y, z, 'YXZ']}` work, and so do three's methods on a node in a `useFrame`: `position.lerp()`, `rotation.order`, `quaternion.slerp()`, `lookAt()`, `getWorldPosition()`, `matrixAutoUpdate`, `material.color.set()`. Every node has three's `Object3D` surface, and the classes and math come from `react-pixi-fiber/three` (see [The three entry](three-entry.md)).
- **three.js itself.** Code that imports `three` builds three.js objects, and so does `@react-three/drei`. A pixi-3d scene can't hold them. Pointing `three` at `react-pixi-fiber/three` covers an app's own imports of the classes that entry has.
- **Sheared transforms.** A mesh turned inside an unevenly scaled parent is lit as if its surface weren't sheared. pixi-3d turns normals with the world transform, dividing each axis by its squared length, which is three's normal matrix only while the axes stay at right angles. Where they don't, a face can be lit at the wrong angle: about a quarter of a sheared box's pixels differ from three's picture. Uniform scales, and uneven scales on the mesh itself, match.
- **`renderOrder`, `frustumCulled` and `layers`.** `renderOrder` and `frustumCulled` are kept and inert: pixi-3d orders draws by pipeline and depth, and its view culls every mesh by its own `cull` setting. There are no `Layers`: objects and cameras have no `layers` to filter by.
- **Cameras.** Cameras project as three's do, from the settings `updateProjectionMatrix` last saw: their own `aspect`, `zoom`, film offset, off-centre orthographic bounds and `setViewOffset`. `camera.view` is pixi-3d's view matrix, so the view offset isn't readable there.
- **Occlusion.** pixi-3d has no occlusion queries, so `onOccluded` never fires and `onVisible` counts every object in the frustum as unoccluded, as r3f does on WebGL.

### Events

- **Picking.** r3f's event system runs unchanged on this package's `Raycaster`, which ports three's `Mesh.raycast`: every triangle the ray crosses is a hit, with the material's `side`, `face`, `barycoord`, `uv` and the blended vertex `normal`. Points, lines and sprites pick within `raycaster.params` thresholds, with `index` and `distanceToRay`, and an `instancedMesh` picks each instance with its `instanceId`. The raycaster has no `layers`, and picks a skinned or morphed mesh in its rest pose.
- **`onDoubleClick` fires.** r3f v10 matches a click to its pointerdown by `pointerId`, and Chrome's `dblclick` has none, so r3f's own build never fires `onDoubleClick`; this package's event layer gives it the pointerdown's. As in r3f, an object's `onPointerCancel` and `onLostPointerCapture` are typed but never called.

### Objects and materials

- **Geometries.** Every geometry is built by three's generator, with three's positions, normals, texture coordinates and groups. three flips an image when it loads it, so a texture's top is at `v = 1`; the geometries hand pixi-3d `1 - v` instead, which shows images the same way up. `ShapeGeometry`, `ExtrudeGeometry` and `TubeGeometry` aren't here yet.
- **Materials.** See [Objects, properties and arguments](objects.md). Several materials on one mesh, `wireframe`, `LineDashedMaterial`, `MeshDepthMaterial` and the physical maps aren't drawn yet. No material takes `displacementMap`, `bumpMap`, `lightMap` or an `envMap` of its own: pixi-3d has no displacement, bump or light maps, and the environment is the scene's. The standard and physical materials keep them as fields that do nothing, so a displaced sphere, as r3f's loading-textures tutorial ends with, compiles and draws as a plain one. A transparent material with `side={DoubleSide}` draws in one pass, in triangle order, where three draws its back faces and then its front faces: where a back face comes later in the geometry it blends over the front, streaking the surface, as three's `forceSinglePass` does. A physical feature (clear coat, sheen, transmission, iridescence, specular, anisotropy) turns on only if it's set before the material is first drawn. The toon material lights a face turned away from every light with ambient light alone, where three still reads the darkest level of the ramp. pixi-3d's sheen has no ambient sheen, and dims the surface under it by its own fit of the sheen's albedo from the view angle alone, where three r187 dims each light's share by the larger of the view's and the light's, so a sheen face seen at a grazing angle comes out darker. Its anisotropy takes its tangent frame differently.
- **Metal.** The standard and physical materials replace pixi-3d's lighting with three's, multi-scatter energy compensation and the energy-conserving ambient light included, so metal lit by lights matches three's pictures. The one approximation is three's 16 by 16 DFG table, worked out from the analytic fit three used before it: a rough metal's highlight comes out a few levels dimmer. Under an environment map, see "Environments".
- **Lines and points.** Lines are 1 pixel wide, and WebGPU puts some of a line's pixels one over from WebGL's and three's. pixi-3d draws no sized points, so each point is a camera-facing square, sized as three sizes `gl_PointSize`; without `sizeAttenuation` the size is in the canvas's pixels.
- **Instancing.** `instancedMesh` draws its instances as slots of pixi-3d's `BatchedMesh3D`, with three's API. `frustumCulled` culls each instance rather than the whole mesh, `setMatrixAt` and `setColorAt` take effect without `needsUpdate`, and instance colours draw with a copy of the material that multiplies them in, so set `transparent` and `side` before the first `setColorAt`.
- **Small shapes on WebGPU.** WebGPU's multisampling covers the edges of shapes a few pixels across differently from WebGL and three.js. A cloud of boxes 3 px wide differs from three's picture on about 6% of its pixels on WebGPU, and not at all on WebGL.
- **Shaders.** `shaderMaterial` and node materials have no pixi-3d equivalent. pixi-3d extends its materials with shader bits instead.

### Lights, fog and output

- **Lighting.** The catalog has `ambientLight`, `hemisphereLight`, `directionalLight`, `pointLight` and `spotLight`, with three's arguments, intensities and `Color`s, and lit scenes match three.js's pictures. Directional and spot lights aim at their `target` as in three, including three's habit of aiming at the origin while the default target is outside the scene. What pixi-3d lacks: `decay` is always three's default of 2, point lights cast no shadows, a spot light projects no `map`, and there is no `RectAreaLight` or `LightProbe`. A hemisphere light is the view's environment, so it gives way to a scene `environment`, and only the first one lights; it also adds a little to glossy highlights, which three's doesn't.
- **Shadows.** `renderer={{ shadows }}`, `castShadow`, `receiveShadow` and the shadow camera, map size and bias work as in three. pixi-3d keeps one shadow resolution per view, the largest any light asks for, and takes its softness from the `shadows` setting, so `shadow-radius`, `shadow-blurSamples` and `shadow-intensity` change nothing. A spot light's shadow covers its whole cone from 1 unit out, whatever its shadow camera says.
- **Fog.** `<fog>` and `<fogExp2>` match three's, mixed in after the tone curve, except on a material whose `toneMapped` is off, which takes no fog.
- **Tone mapping and output.** Materials end with three's own tone curves, AgX and Neutral included, and three's sRGB transfer function, or none for `LinearSRGBColorSpace`, in place of pixi-3d's curves and 2.2 power. A material whose `toneMapped` is off is encoded with pixi-3d's 2.2 power instead, a few levels lighter than three in the darkest tones.

### Environments and backgrounds

- **Backgrounds.** A colour background clears the canvas, untouched by tone mapping, as in three. A 2D texture is stretched over the canvas as in three; pixi.js filters it in display space, so its sharp edges blend a little differently, and `backgroundIntensity` only dims it. A cube texture, or a texture with an equirectangular `mapping`, is drawn around the camera by this package with three's `backgroundRotation`, `backgroundIntensity` and `backgroundBlurriness`, left out of the tone curve when it is sRGB, as three's is. `<Canvas background>` takes a colour only; use `<Environment background>` for an environment map.
- **Environment lighting.** `<Environment>`, `scene.environment` and `useEnvironment` run on pixi-3d's image-based lighting and match three's pictures, with three's rotation and intensity (see [Environment](environment.md)). Two gaps remain. pixi-3d prefilters an environment into a 10-bit cube, so radiance above 1 clips: the sun of an `.hdr` leaves no highlight on smooth surfaces, and `environmentIntensity` above 1 clips the same way. And on partial metal (metalness between 0 and 1), three r187 mixes a dielectric and a metallic lobe by metalness, where pixi-3d mixes F0 first, so such metal comes out up to a fifth darker; metalness 0 and 1 match. `.exr` files and `ground` aren't supported, and `frames` above 1 prefilters the lighting again on every frame filmed.
- **Render targets.** A target stores its picture encoded for display with pixi-3d's 2.2 gamma, where three stores linear light; materials decode it when they sample it and `readRenderTargetPixels` hands back three's linear bytes, so this only shows in a float target's values. `dispose()` destroys a target for good, and only 8-bit 2D targets can be read back. Each scene's view clears its own depth, so `clearDepth` between two scenes changes nothing. See [Render targets](render-targets.md).

### Textures, loaders and animation

- **Loaders.** `TextureLoader`, `CubeTextureLoader`, `ImageLoader`, `FileLoader`, `LoadingManager` and, in `react-pixi-fiber/addons`, `GLTFLoader`, `HDRLoader` and `RGBELoader` keep three's API and build pixi-3d objects. three's other loaders (OBJ, FBX, EXR, ...) aren't here; convert those models to glTF. `CubeTextureLoader.load` returns nothing and gives the texture to `onLoad`, since pixi builds a cube texture from its faces; `loadAsync` and `useLoader` work as in three.
- **Texture transforms.** pixi-3d has one UV transform per material, so a material's `map` sets `offset`, `repeat`, `rotation` and `center` for all its maps, where three gives each map its own. A texture's `source` is pixi's `TextureSource`; read `image` for the image. `dispose()` frees the GPU copy and keeps the texture usable, as in three.
- **Colour textures.** pixi-3d decodes a material's colour maps from sRGB with a 2.2 gamma after filtering, where three decodes with the sRGB curve before: the edges between two colours, and the grey a mipmap blends from them, come out darker. A texture scene differs from three's picture on 2 to 3% of its pixels at those edges, and a scene of magnified and mipmapped textures on about 14%. The 2.2 decode and three's sRGB encode at the end of the shader don't cancel in the darkest tones either, so a texture's near-black comes out up to 6 levels darker than three's. A colour map decodes whatever its `colorSpace`; r3f marks colour maps sRGB when a prop gives them, so this shows only for a map that reaches a material another way.
- **Cube textures.** A `CubeTexture` matches three's orientation, as a background and as an environment.
- **glTF models.** `GLTFLoader` gives three's `GLTF`, with bones as nodes and `nodes` and `materials` for gltfjsx. Materials are `MeshStandardMaterial`, with their maps, or `MeshPhysicalMaterial` with the factors of the clear coat, sheen, transmission, volume, iridescence, specular, anisotropy and IOR extensions; a material whose extension has a texture of its own keeps pixi-3d's material, without three's fields. Punctual lights are `DirectionalLight`, `PointLight` and `SpotLight`. A skinned mesh draws relative to its parent, so it belongs beside its root bone, where files and gltfjsx put it. pixi-3d morphs a geometry on the first renderer that draws it only: a cached model with morph targets shown in a second `<Canvas>` draws unmorphed, so clear it from `useLoader`'s cache when its canvas unmounts. Draco and meshopt decode with pixi-3d's decoders; loader plugins (`register`) aren't supported.
- **Animation.** `AnimationMixer`, `AnimationAction`, `AnimationClip` and the keyframe tracks match three's numbers, including fades, warps, loop modes and their events. three's `InterpolateSmooth` samples linearly, additive blending plays as normal, and there is no `StringKeyframeTrack`.

## What it adds: pixi.js 2D

r3f has no 2D; react-pixi-fiber adds pixi.js's under r3f's rules. Every pixi.js display class, filter, `GraphicsContext` and `Particle` is an element named after its class with a `2D` suffix (`<sprite2D>`, `<blurFilter2D attach="filters-0" />`), takes `args`, props, piercing and `attach` as three's elements do, and gets r3f's events in the same event system as the 3D. 2D in the canvas draws in screen space, over the 3D or, with a negative `zIndex`, under it. See [Your first 2D scene](../two-d/__docs__/your-first-2d-scene.md) and [The screen layer and mixing with 3D](../two-d/__docs__/screen-layer.md). What's different from pixi.js on its own:

- pixi's `EventSystem` is off: r3f's handlers replace `on('pointerdown')` and `onpointerdown`, and every handler under the pointer runs, front to back, until one stops propagation, where pixi delivers to the topmost hit only.
- A 2D element can't be inside a 3D object yet, nor a 3D one inside 2D; either is left out with a warning.
- The root's frameloop draws, not pixi's `Ticker`, and an `<animatedSprite2D>` plays on the root's frames too, unless its `autoUpdate` is `true`.
- A few methods are props as well: `cacheAsTexture`, `renderLayer` (`layer.attach`), `mask` with pixi's mask options (`setMask`), and an animated sprite's `playing`.
- Culling is pixi's `CullerPlugin`, registered with `extensions.add` as for an `Application`; each canvas then culls its 2D before it draws.
- There is no three.js picture to hold 2D to: its scenes are held to their own WebGL and WebGPU references.

## What the package exports

The package exports what r3f v10 does, under the same names, typed against pixi-3d. `Vector3`, `Euler`, `Color` and the other math types are what a prop takes, as in r3f. three's classes, with the same names, come from `react-pixi-fiber/three` (see [The three entry](three-entry.md)). Left out until they work on pixi-3d:

- The multi-canvas registry: `registerPrimary`, `getPrimary`, `waitForPrimary`, `hasPrimary`, `unregisterPrimary` and `getPrimaryIds`.
- The types of three's WebGL and WebGPU renderers and of choosing between them, such as `WebGPURootState` and `LegacyRenderer`. `RootState`, `useThree` and `useFrame` are typed with `PixiRenderer`.
- The element types over three classes pixi-3d doesn't have: `Layers`, `GeometryTransformProps`, `GeometryProps`, `NodeProps`, `TSLNodeInput`, `Object3DMethod` and `RaycastableRepresentation`.
